# Excel & Advanced Spreadsheets

## Summary

Excel/Sheets is one of my strongest practical tools — my spreadsheet capability is significantly beyond basic usage. **Strong → Advanced operationally.**

## How I use spreadsheets

As databases, trackers, reporting systems, dashboards, workflow interfaces, analytical tools, and automation backends — not just for storing numbers.

## Skills

Advanced formulas, data validation, structured trackers, multi-sheet systems, KPI calculations, dashboards, data transformation, scenario analysis, automated reporting, file optimization, formula-driven models.

## The instinct that matters more than the formulas

I understand when a spreadsheet has gone too far, and I've repeatedly worked on migrating processes along this path:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Spreadsheet] --> B[Automated spreadsheet]
    B --> C[Structured database]
    C --> D[Application]
    D --> E[Automated platform]
```

Recognizing *when* to make that jump — not defaulting to "just add another sheet" — is a genuinely useful product/technology instinct, and it's the same instinct behind [[Databases & Data Architecture]] and [[Automation & Workflow Engineering]].

## My Take

Spreadsheets are where a lot of my other skills started — the KPI/dashboard work in [[Data Analytics & BI]], the automation patterns in [[Automation & Workflow Engineering]] via Apps Script, and the database instincts in [[Databases & Data Architecture]] all trace back to having pushed Excel/Sheets past where most people stop.
